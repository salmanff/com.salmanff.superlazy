superlazy - Be Lazy like Linus


A to-do list that keeps itself up to date

superlazy reads your email, Slack conversations and AI coding-agent sessions, and maintains a to-do list for you. It uses your own LLM API key to work out what each message means for you: what you owe, what others owe you, what your team owes, and what is just there to read. It creates to-dos, assigns them and resolves them as it reads each message.

Your mail, your server, your key

An app that reads all your mail should not need to send it to a company you have never met. superlazy is a freezr app, so it has no backend of its own. Your messages, the to-dos and people profiles it builds, and every model call go through your freezr server and your LLM account. freezr is what makes that safe:

  • Your storage. Everything superlazy writes lives in your freezr data space, on your own computer or in the storage you choose. You can export it, or delete it, at any time.
  • The app never sees your passwords. freezr holds the Gmail, Microsoft and Slack sign-ins and makes the calls on the app's behalf. superlazy only gets what you grant.
  • You grant each permission. Reading mail, sending replies, reading Slack and running in the background are separate switches in the app's settings. Each one can be turned off again.
  • Read-only by default. Nothing is sent from your accounts unless you press Send.
  • Your own LLM key. Analysis runs on your Claude or ChatGPT account, at your provider's prices, and the setup screens show what each call cost.

How it works

When a new email or Slack message arrives, superlazy sends it to your LLM together with what it already knows: your current projects and their open to-dos, the people involved and what they usually handle, and any standing instructions you have written ("invoices from [this sender] are low priority"). The model answers with changes: a new to-do, a hand-off to a colleague, a due date, or checking off a to-do that is now done. superlazy applies them and keeps a link back to the message behind each change. Based on your history and the people involved in a thread, your LLM prioritizes each project and to-do (priority 1 thru 5) so you can focus on what's important, or review by due date (where relevant).

If a to-do is resolved offline, you can of course manually check it (and edit priorties and descriptions if you like.) A weekly clean-up process takes a more holistic view to organize your work for the week, proposing merges and closures, if need be.

Install

You need a freezr account and an API key from Claude or ChatGPT. Pick whichever of these fits:

  1. Running your own freezr server

    If you are a dev and want superlazy to monitor Claude Code or Codex, then you need to run on localhost - see dev-setup for details.

  2. using freezr online

    You can also create your freezr server on a cloud provider like AWS, GCP or Azure. See freezr install for details.

    You can use a limited account on freezr.info though it may not allocate enough storage to make superlazy useful. Try it here if you like. Once your account is created you land on an install screen; press Install. To run background processes, you would also need to add a serverless credentials.

After installing, freezr takes you to the app's settings page, where you grant its permissions.

Grant only what you need

Permissions are granted on the app's settings page (/account/app/settings/com.salmanff.superlazy) and can be revoked there at any time. Only the first one is required.

Connect your accounts

Everything superlazy reads comes through connections you add once, on your freezr Account Resources page (/account/resources). Start with your LLM key: under LLM API Keys, press Add LLM Key and paste a Claude or ChatGPT key. On Claude, superlazy uses the small, fast model (haiku); on ChatGPT it uses your default model. Then add at least one input:

Email

Gmail or Microsoft 365 / Outlook: press Connect a Gmail, Microsoft or Slack Account, pick the provider and sign in. Under Services to enable, tick Mail. Choose Read only if you only want to-dos, or Read + Write if you also want to send replies from superlazy.

Other mailboxes (Yahoo, iCloud, Fastmail…): press Add IMAP Mailbox, pick a provider preset and enter your address and an app password from your mail provider.

To learn more about setting up oauth so that your server can access your email, see oauth-setup.

Slack

Press Connect a Gmail, Microsoft or Slack Account, choose Slack (Messaging) and sign in to your workspace. (If you run your own Slack app, Add Slack (paste token) takes a user OAuth token instead.) If your freezr server offers Live updates on the connection, turn them on so new messages are picked up as they arrive. Without them, superlazy checks on each run instead.

Then, in superlazy, open Setup & Prefs → Sources and choose the conversations to track. Only those are read.

To learn more about setting up oauth so that your server can access your Slack messages, see oauth-setup.


Claude Code and Codex sessions (freezr on your own computer)

If you have freezr running on localhost, on the same computer as your coding agents, superlazy can also read the session transcripts they leave behind, and turn the work you asked for and left unfinished into to-dos, in the same projects as your mail. This needs an admin account on that local server:

  1. Allow local folders
    As admin, turn on local_fs_access_enabled in the freezr admin preferences.
  2. Add the folders
    On Account Resources, under File Stores, press Add Local Folder once per agent: ~/.claude/projects for Claude Code, ~/.codex/sessions for Codex.
  3. Grant and choose
    Grant read_work_sessions and pick the folder(s). In superlazy's Setup & Prefs, exclude any repos you don't want read.

Session files are read in place. superlazy never writes to them.

First run

Open superlazy. The setup screen asks which mailbox to learn from and how many days of history to read. More days gives a fuller picture of your work, at the cost of a bigger first call. Add a line of context about your job if you like: it makes the first pass noticeably better. Then press Read my mail & show me what you find, and keep the tab open until it finishes.

superlazy comes back with your projects, the people you work with, and a few questions. Correct anything it got wrong, answer the questions, and press Save and start using SuperLazy. From then on, each new message is analysed as it arrives.

Background processing

superlazy works with the browser open, but it is most useful when it keeps reading while you are away. That is a freezr background job. Grant run_training_job and schedule_training_job, and choose where the job runs when you grant them:

  • On the freezr server. The server's admin has to trust the job first. If freezr runs on your own computer you are the admin, but the job only runs while the computer is on.
  • In your own cloud. Add AWS credentials under Compute Providers (Serverless) on Account Resources, and the job runs in your own AWS account. This works on any freezr server.

Then switch on Check mail in the background in superlazy's Setup & Prefs → Preferences.
